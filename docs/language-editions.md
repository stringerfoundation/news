# Language editions

On the public news homepage, the Cloudflare Worker opens the highest-ranked supported language edition from the browser’s preferences and defaults to English. The language picker remembers an explicit choice for one year in a browser cookie. Direct links to localized editions open the linked edition.

The Pages build includes `/fr/` (French), `/es/` (Spanish), `/ar/` (Arabic), `/zh/` (Simplified Chinese), `/ru/` (Russian), `/nl/` (Dutch), and `/de/` (German). Each edition has its own pinned publisher catalogue in `sources/`; source selection is saved separately for each language. Arabic uses a right-to-left interface; story text keeps its original direction.

It also includes `/br/` (Brazilian Portuguese) and `/pt/` (European Portuguese), sharing the pinned catalogue in `portuguese-sources.ts`. On the project site these resolve beneath `/stringer-news/`. The edition links and shared assets remain relative. The locale pages preserve the same UTC build stamp as the English reader.

These editions translate the interface only; headlines and Stringer reporting remain in their original languages. The hourly publisher reads Atomstr and checks each mirror signature and RSS source. Readers fetch verified headlines from their edition’s headlines.json file on the CDN. Publisher summaries and photos are discarded. Language editions look up reusable illustrations using the original headline and their Wikipedia language, and show source and license details in Credits. Mirror timestamps and social-feed fallbacks are labeled, and unavailable mirrors retain previously verified stories without claiming a successful refresh. BR and PT share source choices in browser storage; other language choices remain independent. The English reader continues to enable every configured global publisher. The checked publication build collects Kiswahili headlines from DW, RFI, HabariLeo, BBC and VOA. Those sources update with each published build, and their collection times appear in the feed status. The reader displays publisher headlines, publication timestamps and original links.

Each published Atomstr edition also includes up to eight verified headlines per source in its own HTML. Readers can display these headlines while the published edition file loads, and keep them if the connection fails. The reader gives newer browser collections priority and ignores build headlines older than seven days. The Pages build on `main` refreshes the published data hourly.

The catalogues use active feeds listed by [Atomstr](https://atomstr.data.haus/), checked on 19 October 2026. They do not claim exhaustive coverage. Arabic currently has two direct publisher mirrors; Chinese includes both simplified and traditional reporting and a clearly named Cantonese source. Dutch includes explicitly named football feeds alongside general news; Spanish currently includes EL PAÍS and the sports publisher Olé. All editions preserve the publisher’s original wording and script. A catalogue listing is not a guarantee of continued relay availability.

The English edition also has a cached Atomstr fallback for Al Jazeera and DW. The publication build verifies the pinned mirrors against the same publisher RSS URLs. It embeds headline text, original links and timestamps in the homepage. The reader uses cached entries up to 36 hours old during a primary outage or to fill known gaps, with primary headlines taking precedence for matching links. Fallback headlines and their timestamps carry Atomstr attribution. Feed status retains the primary relay counts and reports any cached additions separately.

Run `pnpm run build`, `pnpm test`, and `pnpm run test:browser` to check the generated pages and browser behavior. Locale routes are available on GitHub Pages after merge and deployment. The apex homepage links to those editions on GitHub Pages; the Cloudflare Worker continues to serve the homepage and reader assets only.
